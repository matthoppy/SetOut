SetOut · Brand identity · Version 1 · November 2026

Every set‑out starts from a point

SetOut makes software for the glass industry. Before any pane is cut, someone sets it out: they mark a point, measure a line from it, and order glass to a size. The identity is built from those three things, so it belongs to the trade it serves and looks like none of the companies in it.

Recommendation

Point, drawn in Line's language

Point is the identity. Line becomes the drawing language for everything professional users print, check and send to a factory. Edge stays as the reason the brand is mint.

  • It looks established on day one. Six letters and one square. Brands that have been around for years reduce to a name and one owned detail; startups reach for symbols.
  • The point works for a living. In the product it marks the step you are on, the field you are filling in and the figure that matters. The logo's detail and the interface's signal are the same thing.
  • It holds at every size. The app icon is the first letter and the point. It reads in a 16 px browser tab and on the side of a van, with no second logo to manage.
  • It leaves the industry open. There is no pane, door or shower in it, so SetOut Balustrades and SetOut Connect sit under it as naturally as SetOut Showers.
  • Line covers its one weakness. Point is light on technical depth. Dimension lines, drawing ticks, mono figures and title blocks give the sheets, quotes and drawings the authority a factory expects.

Drawing language

Borrowed from Direction C and used wherever SetOut shows a size. It gives the product its technical authority without a single extra colour.

  1. Shape
  2. Measure

690 × 1980 mm

Point

Marks the one thing that matters now: the step you're on, the field being filled in, the size just worked out. Never more than one per view.

Line

Dimension lines with 45° ticks, never arrowheads. One-pixel lines, the figure above the line, units said once per drawing.

Grid

A faint drafting grid behind drawings and technical screens only. Never behind paragraphs, never on marketing photography.

Title block

Bottom right of every drawing: what it is, which pane, the glass, the scale, the revision and who checked it.

Voice

Confident, clear and specific, in the words the trade already uses. SetOut sounds like the best estimator you've worked with.

Sizes

Width first, then height: 690 × 1980 mm. A space before mm, the × sign and never the letter x, whole millimetres, and degrees with no space: 90°.

Write it like this

  • Measure the left wall at the floor.Input left wall bottom measurement value.
  • We'll check every size before glass is ordered.Our AI-powered engine guarantees perfect accuracy.
  • Send to the toughenerExport fabrication data
  • That door would weigh 52 kg. Two Vienna hinges hold 45 kg, so it needs three.Error: weight limit exceeded.

Short sentences, British spelling, and the trade's own words: pane, toughened, cut-out, notch, hinge side, set-out.
